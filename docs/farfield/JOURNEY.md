# Far Field: the journey (Josh, 7 Oct 2026, verbatim)

Use Inside as a reference for pacing, environmental storytelling and carefully staged encounters. Create our own events around the rabbit's abilities and perspective.
The overall journey should move from recognisable outskirts into an increasingly unfamiliar industrial world, then towards an uncertain open landscape. Avoid explaining everything with dialogue or written lore. Let the player observe human behaviour and draw conclusions.
Use this encounter rhythm flexibly:
Quiet exploration → a clue that something is wrong → observing a threat → taking action → a consequence or discovery → breathing space.
Proposed journey:

1. THE VERGE
The rabbit explores rain-soaked grass beside an enormous concrete boundary. Teach running, listening and squeezing through gaps. A vehicle stops beyond the wall. Footsteps approach. The rabbit slips through a drain before the person reaches them.
2. THE COURTYARD
Introduce the box-pushing puzzle in a quiet space. While the player experiments, a distant door opens and a human crosses an elevated walkway. Nothing attacks yet. Establish that activity continues beyond the rabbit's immediate path.
3. THE SEARCH
A human enters the rabbit's area. Show the light and footsteps before the danger arrives. The player watches from shelter, crosses exposed ground and escapes through a gap. If spotted, the human pursues and can catch or shoot the rabbit. Use restrained, non-graphic failure and a nearby checkpoint.
4. THE WORKS
Shift from pursuit to environmental puzzles. A large machine cycles through movement and stillness. The rabbit must learn the rhythm, find shelter and cross during a safe interval. Show the mechanism operating before asking the player to risk entering it.
5. THE OCCUPIED BUILDING
Humans move through distant rooms in an unnervingly coordinated routine. Their purpose remains unclear. Some ignore the rabbit; one notices. Build tension around uncertainty about whether being seen will lead to danger.
6. THE DESCENT
The route ahead closes and the rabbit must travel deeper through maintenance spaces. Use narrow passages, shifting platforms and rising water as environmental threats. Provide readable escape routes; do not require the rabbit to swim long underwater sections or operate human controls.
7. THE FALSE EXIT
The rabbit reaches grass and daylight, but the apparent outside space lies within another enormous structure. Give the player a genuine quiet interval before revealing this. It should change their understanding of the place rather than simply spring a trap.
8. THE CROSSING
Combine previously learned abilities in one final pursuit: hide, push through a loose obstruction, squeeze beneath a barrier and cross exposed ground. Make the sequence demanding but readable, with checkpoints between major beats.
9. THE FAR FIELD
End with space and stillness. The rabbit reaches open ground and gradually relaxes. Leave distant signs of the human world visible, but avoid a final cheap attack. We do not need to explain the whole society to understand why reaching this place matters.

These are narrative beats, not a requirement to build nine large levels immediately. First complete one short sequence that combines exploration, a rabbit-specific puzzle, a human encounter and relief.
Keep the rabbit an individual throughout. Include small behaviours such as listening, sniffing and settling when safe. Do not turn other animals into puzzle tools or enemies. The player's connection should grow through inhabiting the rabbit's life, not only witnessing threats to it.
